# ADR-0003: Power system choices

- **Status:** accepted by the owner, 2026-11-15, with changes (15 A fuse, charge-while-running analysis). Module choices are re-checked in Phase 5 before the BOM is final.
- **Spec:** §6, D17, D18
- **Numbers:** [power_budget.md](../design/power_budget.md), generated by `tools/power_budget.py`

The power *concept* (2S2P 18650 + BMS + DC jack, everything in the base) is the owner's decision and is unchanged. These are the choices the spec left open.

## 1. Servo rail: 6.0 V set-point, graceful sag near empty

**Problem.** A 2S pack runs from 8.4 V down to about 6.6 V in use. A buck converter needs its input above its output, so 6.0 V cannot be held all the way down. The common "8 A / 300 W" modules make this worse: the XL4016 datasheet lists 8 V minimum input in some revisions, and listings for the 20 A / 300 W module quote up to 3 V dropout.

**Decision.**
- Set-point 6.0 V, from a converter that accepts input down to 4.5–6 V and runs to about 100 % duty:
  - default: **"12 A 4.5–30 V adjustable buck module"** (generic, heatsinked, 8 A continuous);
  - alternative: an **RC "UBEC" rated 10 A or more, 2S–6S input, 6.0 V output**.
- The rail may sag to about **5.4 V** in the last part of the charge. All servos are rated 4.8–6 V; the cost is up to about 7 % torque.
- **Firmware thresholds match that sag:** warning at 7.0 V (3.5 V/cell), park and refuse motion at 6.6 V (3.3 V/cell), where even a poor converter still delivers 5.4 V. The measurement is low-pass filtered (3 s) with 0.2 V hysteresis so current spikes do not trigger a park. Values live in `arm_params.json → power.firmware_thresholds`.
- **The module's dropout must be measured on the bench** before the base is closed (`unverified.md`, item 3).

Rejected: a permanently lower rail (gives away torque all the time). A 3S pack would remove the problem but changes D17, so it is not proposed.

## 2. Logic rail

Separate small 5 V buck (MP1584 or Mini360 class, ≥ 1 A) feeding the ESP32 5V pin **through a Schottky diode (1N5819)**, buck set to about 5.3 V to make up for the diode drop. With the diode, plugging in USB while the pack is on cannot push current back into the buck. To be checked against the actual DevKit schematic in Phase 5.

## 3. Charging, and charging while running

- **12 V DC wall adapter (≥ 2 A, 5.5 × 2.1 mm, EU plug) → DC jack → CC/CV buck module (XL4015 CC/CV class) set to 8.2 V / 1.5 A → BMS charge input.**
- Why a module inside rather than an "8.4 V Li-ion charger" brick: 12 V adapters are everywhere, a wrong adapter (9–24 V) does no harm, and setting the two trimmers with a multimeter is a good team exercise. It must be set *before* the pack is connected; the wiring guide makes this a checklist step.

**The owner's concern, checked:** a CC/CV module has no end-of-charge detection at all. It holds its set voltage for as long as the adapter is plugged in, whether or not the arm is running. So the pack *is* held at the set voltage indefinitely. That cannot overcharge (voltage-limited, plus BMS per-cell cut-off), but sitting at 4.2 V per cell for long periods ages Li-ion cells.

**Made safe by:**
1. **Set-point 8.2 V (4.10 V per cell) instead of 8.4 V.** This is a normal stand-by level for packs that stay on a supply. Cost: about 10 % of capacity.
2. Firmware reports "charger connected / full" above 8.1 V; the app shows a reminder to unplug.
3. Team rule: unplug when full, never charge unattended.

**Power path rejected for v1:** the servos pull peaks far beyond what a 12 V / 2 A adapter delivers, so the pack must stay in the circuit; and a diode-OR costs 0.4–0.5 V in the battery line, exactly the headroom the servo rail lacks near empty.

**Side effect, balancing:** common 2S balance boards only bleed a cell at 4.20 V, so at 4.10 V they stay inactive. Mitigation: four cells of the same type and batch, and a multimeter check of both cell voltages every few months. Optionally the free ADC pin can watch the pack mid-point.

**Power-bank input (USB-C 5 V → 2S boost charger, IP2326 class):** optional module with a blanked panel cut-out; final yes/no in Phase 5. This module *does* terminate on low current and a running arm confuses it, so it may only be used with the arm parked and disabled.

## 4. Main switch, fuse, wiring, BMS

- **Rocker directly in the battery line: KCD3 (15 A) or a larger KCD4.** No MOSFET load switch: fewer parts, fewer ways to get it wrong. A KCD1 (6 A) is not acceptable.
- **Fuse: 15 A automotive blade fuse** in an inline holder at the pack (owner change from 10 A). The all-stall peak is about 10 A and the bulk capacitors add inrush at switch-on, so a 10 A fuse could blow in normal faults it should ride through. 15 A matches the rocker and still clears a short instantly.
- **Main power wiring: 16 AWG or thicker** (battery → fuse → switch → converters, and the servo rail to the distribution board), so the fuse always protects the wire.
- **BMS: 2S, 20 A, with balancing**, above the fuse rating so the fuse handles hard faults.
- **Cells:** 4 × genuine high-drain 18650 in cell holders (no soldering to cells). Baseline 2500 mAh; **3000 mAh high-drain preferred if available** (about 20 % more runtime).

## 5. Battery sensing

100 kΩ / 33 kΩ divider + 100 nF into an ADC1 pin, taken after the main switch. 8.4 V reads as 2.08 V, inside the linear ADC range. Below 4.5 V with USB connected, the firmware concludes servo power is off and refuses to enable.
